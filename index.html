About

Mohd Hamza Naim Shaikh is a Research Fellow at Queen's University Belfast. His research is on the physical layer of beyond-5G and 6G networks, and in particular on hardware that can reshape the wireless channel: reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS), simultaneously transmitting and reflecting surfaces (STAR-RIS), and fluid and movable antennas. He studies how these devices work with non-orthogonal and rate-splitting multiple access, integrated sensing and communication, and physical-layer security, and how they behave when transceivers are imperfect. His work combines communication theory, stochastic analysis and optimization with learning-based methods.

Before joining Queen's, he was a Research Fellow at University College London (2025–2026), working on fluid reconfigurable intelligent surfaces and fluid-antenna multiple access, and a Postdoctoral Research Assistant at Nazarbayev University in Astana, Kazakhstan (2022–2025), where he worked on STAR-RIS and aerial-RIS-assisted NOMA and on ISAC, and co-supervised undergraduate researchers. He received his PhD in Communication Engineering from IIIT-Delhi in 2022 as a Visvesvaraya PhD Fellow; his thesis on residual hardware impairments in future wireless systems received the institute's Best PhD Dissertation Award in 2023. He holds M.Tech and B.Tech degrees from Aligarh Muslim University.

He reviews for IEEE Transactions on Wireless Communications, IEEE Transactions on Vehicular Technology, IEEE Internet of Things Journal and IEEE Wireless Communications Letters, among others, and serves on the technical program committees of IEEE ICC, VTC and ANTS.

Research

Seven threads run through the work. Each one links to the papers behind it.

  • Reconfigurable intelligent surfaces

    How to partition, assign and place RIS and STAR-RIS so that one surface can serve many users, from dual-IRS outdoor-to-indoor links to multi-STAR-RIS NOMA and UAV-mounted aerial RIS.

    Read the tutorial

  • Fluid and movable antennas

    Antennas and surfaces whose elements can change position: performance analysis and sum-rate optimization for fluid-RIS-enabled NOMA, and port selection for compact ultra-massive antenna arrays.

    Read the tutorial

  • Next-generation multiple access

    Grant-free and cooperative NOMA, user clustering, RIS partitioning and power control, and rate-splitting multiple access for massive connectivity.

    Read the tutorial

  • Sensing and physical-layer security

    Using the sensing signal of an ISAC system to jam eavesdroppers whose location is uncertain, and using RIS to protect vehicular and indoor links.

    Read the tutorial

  • Hardware-impaired transceivers

    What residual hardware impairments, phase noise and amplifier nonlinearity do to RIS-aided, NOMA and OFDM systems. This was the subject of his PhD thesis.

    Read the tutorial

  • UAV and vehicular networks

    3D deployment and partitioning of aerial RIS, deep reinforcement learning for resource allocation, and V2I, V2V and D2D links under Nakagami-m fading.

    Read the tutorial

  • Full-duplex relaying

    Antenna allocation and self-energy recycling for energy-efficient full-duplex MIMO relays, and the energy-spectral efficiency trade-off.

Tutorials

Short introductions to the ideas behind this research, each with the key equations, a diagram and an interactive model. They are written for students and engineers who are new to the area.

  • 6G networks

    What 6G is meant to deliver, how its targets compare with 5G, and where standardisation stands.

  • Reconfigurable intelligent surfaces

    Panels of passive elements that reflect with controllable phase, so the network can shape the channel itself.

  • Non-orthogonal multiple access

    Several users share one time slot and band, separated by power and successive interference cancellation.

  • Integrated sensing and communication

    One radio, one band and often one signal that both carries data and senses the surroundings.

  • Vehicular communications

    V2V, V2I, V2P and V2N links, and why speed makes the channel change within a millisecond.

  • Fluid antenna systems

    Antennas that switch position within a few wavelengths to find a strong spot in the fading pattern.

  • Hardware impairments

    Why distortion that grows with the signal puts a ceiling on every link, and what it means for RIS and NOMA.

  • Optimization for wireless networks

    How networks choose powers, beams and phases, and the methods used when the problem is not convex.

Collaboration and supervision

I welcome collaboration with researchers in academia and industry on the physical layer of 6G, especially:

  • optimization and performance analysis of RIS, STAR-RIS, fluid-antenna and movable-antenna systems
  • UAV-enabled and aerial-RIS networks
  • non-orthogonal and rate-splitting multiple access
  • integrated sensing and communication, and physical-layer security
  • design that accounts for transceiver hardware impairments
  • learning-based methods for resource allocation, such as deep reinforcement learning

I am glad to co-supervise PhD, master’s and undergraduate students, and to host visiting researchers and interns. If you are interested, email me your CV, a few lines on the topic you would like to work on, and, if you have one, a paper or report you have written.

Visiting researchers and interns usually need their own funding, for example a scholarship or an exchange programme.
