Hardware impairments

Real radios add distortion that grows with the signal itself. Raising the power can’t remove it, so it sets a ceiling on every link.

The idea in 30 seconds

Imperfect blocksAmplifiers, oscillators, mixers and converters all distort.

A hard ceilingSNDR saturates at 1/κ², so capacity stops growing.

Where the distortion comes from

Every block in a radio is a little imperfect. Calibration removes most of the damage, but never all of it; what remains is called residual hardware impairment.

What it does to the symbols

Each impairment bends the constellation in its own way. On top of thermal noise, these errors move points even when the signal is strong.

One number: κ

Modelling every imperfection separately is hard. A simple model works remarkably well: the residual distortion behaves like extra Gaussian noise, with power proportional to the signal.

Here is the transmitter’s distortion and the receiver’s. The level κ is the error vector magnitude (EVM) that datasheets and standards quote.

The ceiling

Turn the power up and the noise becomes negligible, but the distortion grows with the signal. The ratio stops improving at 1/κ², and the capacity stops at a ceiling.

Key idea

Capacity is capped at log₂(1 + 1/κ²), however strong the signal. With an EVM of 8%, the ceiling is 21.9 dB of SNDR, or 7.3 bit/s/Hz.

Things to try:

  • Choose the 256-QAM EVM limit. The ceiling rises to about 9.7 bit/s/Hz, still well short of an ideal receiver at high SNR.
  • Look at low SNR. Below about 10 dB the curves are almost identical: noise dominates and impairments hardly matter.

What it means for new technologies

Impairments shape how far each 6G technique can go.

Massive MIMOArrays tolerate cheaper hardware: distortion adds incoherently while the signal adds coherently.
Relays and RISImpairments accumulate over two hops; phase errors at RIS elements act like phase noise on every path.NOMAImperfect cancellation leaves residual interference, so every user meets a ceiling.
New waveformsAmplifier nonlinearity regrows the out-of-band emission that windowed OFDM removes.

These effects are the subject of the PhD thesis Performance Analysis of Future Wireless Communication Systems under Residual Hardware Impairments (IIIT-Delhi, 2022) and the papers listed below.

What designers can do

  • strategySpend where it bindsAt high SNR, better hardware (lower κ) helps more than more power.
  • optimisationDesign with the modelInclude κ in power allocation, phase optimisation and scheduling.
  • DSPCompensate digitallyPredistortion, phase-noise tracking and IQ calibration.
  • arraysUse more antennasSpreading power over many antennas dilutes each one’s distortion.
Key takeaways
  • Residual impairments behave like noise proportional to the signal power, with a level κ equal to the EVM.
  • SNDR saturates at 1/κ² and capacity at log₂(1 + 1/κ²).
  • At high SNR, better hardware beats more power.
  • Impairments accumulate across hops and limit relaying, RIS and NOMA; large arrays dilute them.

Check your understanding

A link has κ = 5%. What is its maximum spectral efficiency?

1/κ² = 400, or 26 dB, so the ceiling is log₂(401) ≈ 8.65 bit/s/Hz.

Why does raising the transmit power not remove the distortion?

The distortion power grows with the signal power, so their ratio stays fixed.

Why can massive MIMO use lower-quality hardware?

Distortion from different antennas is largely uncorrelated and adds incoherently, while the wanted signal adds coherently, so its relative impact shrinks as the array grows.
