Hardware impairments

Real transceivers add distortion that grows with the signal itself. Raising the transmit power does not remove it, so it sets a ceiling on what any link can achieve.

Where the distortion comes from

Every radio chain is imperfect, in a few well-known ways.

Power amplifiers
They become nonlinear near saturation, compressing peaks and spreading energy into neighbouring channels. OFDM signals, with their high peak-to-average power ratio, suffer most.
Oscillators
Phase noise makes the carrier’s phase drift randomly. It rotates the constellation and, in OFDM, leaks energy between subcarriers.
IQ imbalance
The in-phase and quadrature branches differ slightly in gain and phase, which creates a mirror image of the signal.
Data converters
Digital-to-analogue and analogue-to-digital converters quantise the signal with finite resolution.

Calibration and compensation, such as digital predistortion of the amplifier, remove most of this distortion but never all of it. What remains is called residual hardware impairment.

Key idea

Capacity is capped at bits per channel use, however strong the signal. With an EVM of 8%, the ceiling is 21.9 dB of SNDR, or 7.3 bit/s/Hz.

The presets are the transmitter EVM limits that 3GPP sets for each modulation: 17.5% for QPSK, 12.5% for 16-QAM, 8% for 64-QAM and 3.5% for 256-QAM.

Things to try:

  • Choose the 256-QAM limit. The ceiling rises to about 9.7 bit/s/Hz, still well short of what an ideal receiver gets at high SNR.
  • Look at low SNR. Below about 10 dB the two curves are almost identical, because noise dominates and impairments hardly matter there.

What it means for new technologies

Massive MIMO
Arrays tolerate cheaper hardware than expected. Distortion from different antennas adds incoherently while the signal adds coherently, so an array can trade hardware quality for antenna count.
Relays and RIS
In a two-hop link, impairments from both hops accumulate. A RIS adds no amplifier, but phase errors and coarse phase resolution at its elements act like phase noise on every reflected path.
NOMA
Successive interference cancellation subtracts a reconstructed signal. Distortion makes the reconstruction imperfect, so residual interference remains and both users hit ceilings. See NOMA.
New waveforms
Windowed OFDM (WOLA) cuts out-of-band emission, but amplifier nonlinearity regrows it. Digital predistortion is needed to keep the benefit.

These effects are the subject of the PhD thesis Performance Analysis of Future Wireless Communication Systems under Residual Hardware Impairments (IIIT-Delhi, 2022) and the papers listed below.

What designers can do

  • Spend effort where it binds. At high SNR, better hardware (a lower ) helps more than more transmit power.
  • Design with the model. Power allocation, phase optimisation and user scheduling should include the distortion terms.
  • Compensate digitally. Predistortion, phase-noise tracking and IQ-imbalance estimation.
  • Use arrays. Spreading the signal over many antennas dilutes the distortion of each one.

  • Residual impairments behave like noise proportional to the signal power, with a level equal to the EVM.

Why does raising the transmit power not remove the distortion?

The distortion power grows with the signal power, so the ratio between the two stays fixed.

Why can massive MIMO use lower-quality hardware?

Distortion from different antennas is largely uncorrelated and adds incoherently, while the wanted signal adds coherently. Its relative impact shrinks as the array grows.
