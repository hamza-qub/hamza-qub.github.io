What performance analysis means

Simulations tell you how a system performs for the cases you tried. Analysis gives a formula: it holds for every parameter value, shows which parameters matter, and reveals how performance scales.

The metrics themselves are introduced in performance metrics. Here we see how to derive them.

Start from the fading distribution

Everything follows from the distribution of the channel gain. Rayleigh fading suits rich scattering with no direct path; Nakagami-m and Rician add a stronger, steadier component.

Try it: analysis meets simulation

Every closed form should be checked against a Monte Carlo simulation. The catch: to see an outage of 10⁻⁵, you need well over 10⁵ samples.

Things to try:

  • With 10³ samples, the simulated points stop at about 10⁻³; beyond that, no outage events occur at all.
  • Switch to m = 4. The curve falls four decades per 10 dB, so simulation runs out of events at a much lower SNR.
  • Press New run with few samples. Points at low outage jump around: a rule of thumb is to collect at least 100 events per point.

The analyst’s toolbox

  • Special functionsGamma, Bessel, exponential integral, Meijer-G and Fox-H functions, from the NIST DLMF.
  • Numerical integrationGauss–Laguerre and Gauss–Chebyshev quadrature when no closed form exists.
  • Moment matchingApproximate a hard sum or product by a gamma variable with the same mean and variance.
  • Bounds and asymptoticsWhen the exact result is unwieldy, a tight bound or a high-SNR limit carries the insight.
Key idea

Stochastic geometry models network nodes as a random point process, usually Poisson. Averaging over positions as well as fading gives network-wide results, such as the coverage probability of a whole cellular network, in closed form.

Key takeaways
  • Analysis starts from the distribution of the SNR; outage is its CDF at a threshold.
  • At high SNR, outage behaves as (Gcγ̄)−Gd: diversity sets the slope, coding gain the offset.
  • Fading costs capacity because the logarithm is concave; for Rayleigh about 0.83 bit/s/Hz at high SNR.
  • Monte Carlo checks need many samples at low outage; aim for 100 events per point.

Check your understanding

In Rayleigh fading at 20 dB average SNR, what is the outage probability for a target of 1 bit/s/Hz?

γth = 1, so Pout = 1 − e−1/100 ≈ 0.01.

A scheme’s outage falls from 10⁻² to 10⁻⁶ between 10 and 30 dB. What is its diversity order?

Four decades over 20 dB is two decades per 10 dB: diversity order 2.

How many Monte Carlo samples do you need to estimate an outage of 10⁻⁴ reliably?

About 100 events, so around 10⁶ samples.
