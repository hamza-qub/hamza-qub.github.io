Generate random bits, send them through a model of the world, count what goes wrong. Done carefully, simulation checks your analysis and goes where formulas cannot.

Why simulate

Analysis needs simplifying assumptions; simulation can include whatever the model omits. Most papers use both: a formula for insight, a simulation to check it and to go where the formula cannot.

The Monte Carlo recipe

Generate random data, push it through the system, count what goes wrong, and repeat until the count is trustworthy.

How many samples?

A Monte Carlo estimate is itself random. Its accuracy depends on the number of errors counted, not the number of bits sent.

Try it: three lines that break a simulation

Most wrong curves in papers come from a handful of mistakes. Switch them on and watch the points leave the theory curve.

Things to try:

  • Choose ‘Noise not split over I and Q’. Complex noise of variance N₀ has N₀/2 per real dimension; forgetting the split costs exactly 3 dB.
  • Choose ‘20 log₁₀ for power’. Powers convert with 10 log₁₀; amplitudes with 20. The error stretches the SNR axis by a factor of two.
  • Switch to Rayleigh fading. The curve falls only one decade per 10 dB: diversity order 1.
Key idea

Always plot your simulation on top of a known closed form, such as BPSK in AWGN, before trusting it on a new system.

Getting the numbers right

Simulations in papers are judged on their parameters as much as their code. The noise power is the first thing a reviewer checks.

The link budget tutorial puts this together with path loss and antenna gains. Be explicit about whether an SNR is per bit (Eb/N0) or per symbol, and in which bandwidth.

Tools

Start with your own NumPy or MATLAB code; it teaches you the most. Move to a library when you need standard-compliant detail or scale.

Make it reproducible

  • Fix the seedsRecord the random seeds so that every figure can be regenerated exactly.
  • Parameter tableList every value: bandwidth, noise figure, path-loss model, antenna counts, number of trials.
  • Show the checkInclude a curve where simulation and closed form meet.
  • Share the codeA public repository answers most reviewer questions before they are asked.
Key takeaways
  • Simulate at the level that matches the question: link, system or network.
  • Monte Carlo accuracy depends on the number of errors counted; aim for at least 100 per point.
  • Most bugs are in noise variance, dB conversion or SNR definitions; validate against a closed form.
  • Fix seeds, list every parameter and share the code.

Check your understanding

You simulate BPSK and your curve is exactly 3 dB worse than theory. What is the likely bug?

The noise variance per real dimension was set to N₀ instead of N₀/2.

How many bits do you need to estimate a BER of 10⁻⁵ to about ±20%?

About 100 errors, so around 10⁷ bits.

What is the noise power in a 20 MHz channel with a 9 dB noise figure?

−174 + 73 + 9 = −92 dBm.
