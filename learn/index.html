Wireless tutorials

Picture-first introductions to how wireless works and where 6G is going, in three tracks: foundations, 6G technologies and the tools researchers use.

  • 19 tutorials
  • Diagrams, photos and interactive models
  • Self-checks and links to go further
1

Wireless foundations

How a wireless link works, from bits to antennas: the communication model, propagation, link budgets, antennas, the metrics everyone uses, and what real hardware does.

  • 1. Communication modelThe communication modelEvery wireless link turns bits into a radio wave, sends it through a channel that distorts it and adds noise, and turns it back into bits.9 min · model: Noisy constellations
  • 2. PropagationWireless propagationBetween two antennas, a radio wave spreads out, bounces, bends and scatters. The result is a channel that loses power with distance and fades as you move.10 min · model: Coverage explorer
  • 3. Link budgetLink budgetsA link budget adds up every gain and loss between two radios to answer one question: will the signal arrive strong enough to beat the noise?8 min · model: Link budget calculator
  • 4. AntennasAntennasAntennas turn currents into radio waves and decide where the energy goes. Put many together and you can steer a beam without moving anything.12 min · model: Steer an array
  • 5. Performance metricsPerformance metricsSNR, SINR, BLER, spectral efficiency, outage, bits per joule: the measures that papers, standards and engineers use to say whether a wireless system is any good.8 min · model: Outage explorer
  • 6. Hardware impairmentsHardware impairmentsReal radios add distortion that grows with the signal itself. Raising the power can’t remove it, so it sets a ceiling on every link.9 min · model: Find the capacity ceiling
2

6G technologies

The physical-layer ideas behind 6G: networks that sense, surfaces that shape the channel, radios that talk and listen at once, new spectrum, light, and new ways to share it.

  • 1. ISACIntegrated sensing and communicationOne radio, one band and often one signal that both carries data and senses the surroundings, turning the network into a radar.10 min · model: Resolve two targets
  • 2. RISReconfigurable intelligent surfacesThin panels of many small elements, each able to switch how it reflects. Together they let the network shape the channel, which engineers could once only measure.12 min · model: Steer a reflected beam
  • 3. Full-duplexFull-duplex radiosTransmitting and receiving on the same frequency at the same time could double spectral efficiency, if a radio can stop hearing itself.8 min · model: Cancellation budget
  • 4. THzTerahertz communicationAbove 100 GHz lie tens of gigahertz of unused spectrum: enough for links of hundreds of gigabits per second, if the losses and the hardware can be tamed.8 min
  • 5. VLCVisible light communicationLED lights can flicker data faster than the eye can see, turning every lamp into a wireless access point with hundreds of terahertz of free spectrum.7 min · model: Light up a room
  • 6. RadarRadar signal processingSend a signal, listen for the echo, and turn delays, Doppler shifts and phase differences into a map of what is out there.14 min · model: Tune a CFAR detector
  • 7. NOMA & RSMANOMA and rate-splittingLet several users share the same time and frequency by giving them different power levels, then peel their signals apart at the receiver.10 min · model: Compare NOMA and TDMA rate regions
  • 8. Fluid antennasFluid and movable antennasAn antenna that changes its position within a small space. The channel changes over a fraction of a wavelength, so that freedom buys diversity without extra radio chains.9 min · model: Pick the best port
  • 9. VehicularVehicular communicationsVehicles talking to each other, to the roadside, to pedestrians and to the network. Speed is what makes it hard: the channel can change in a fraction of a millisecond.9 min · model: See Doppler fading
3

Tools

The methods researchers use to design and analyse wireless systems: optimization, performance analysis, machine learning and simulation.

  • 1. OptimizationOptimization for wireless networksHow much power to send, where to point the beams and whom to serve: optimization is the language for making those decisions well, and for proving that they are.14 min · model: Watch SCA climb, and get stuck
  • 2. Performance analysisPerformance analysisTurn a system model into formulas for outage, capacity and error rate: results that hold for every parameter value and show what really matters.12 min · model: Analysis meets simulation
  • 3. Machine learningMachine learning for wirelessWhen the model is wrong, or right but too slow, learn from data. Neural networks can replace hundreds of iterations with one fast pass, if they are trained and tested with care.11 min · model: Train a tiny network
  • 4. SimulationSimulation for wireless researchGenerate random bits, send them through a model of the world, count what goes wrong. Done carefully, simulation checks your analysis and goes where formulas cannot.10 min · model: Find the bug

Each track can be read in order, but every tutorial stands on its own. Corrections and suggestions are welcome at h.shaikh@qub.ac.uk.
