Talking and listening

Almost every radio today is half duplex: it takes turns, in time (TDD) or in frequency (FDD). A full-duplex radio transmits and receives on the same frequency at the same time, which could double the spectral efficiency.

The problem: hearing yourself

A radio’s own transmission arrives at its receiver enormously stronger than the faint signal it is trying to hear.

Key idea

Full duplex is a cancellation problem. Every decibel of self-interference left above the noise floor costs the receiver a decibel of SNR.

Cancelling in stages

No single technique gives 110 dB or more. Designs stack three stages, each removing what it can before the next.

Try it: is full duplex worth it?

Full duplex sends both directions at once, so with perfect cancellation it doubles the rate. Residual self-interference eats into that gain.

Things to try:

  • With the default settings, the total suppression is 110 dB and full duplex beats half duplex by about 40%.
  • Lower the digital cancellation to 30 dB. Residual interference swamps the receiver and full duplex falls far below half duplex.
  • Raise the link SNR. Strong links need more cancellation before full duplex pays off.

Where full duplex helps

relayingRelaysA full-duplex relay receives and forwards at once, avoiding the half-rate penalty of two-hop links.
ISACMonostatic sensingAn ISAC base station must listen for echoes while it transmits.
standardsSBFD in 5G-AdvancedBase stations receive uplink inside a downlink band, cutting latency and boosting coverage.
energySelf-energy recyclingA full-duplex node can harvest energy from its own loopback signal.

Full-duplex relaying with antenna allocation and self-energy recycling was the topic of several of Hamza Shaikh’s papers, listed below.

Key takeaways
  • Half-duplex radios take turns in time or frequency; full duplex does both at once in the same band.
  • Self-interference must be suppressed by around 110 dB or more to reach the noise floor.
  • Designs stack antenna isolation, analog cancellation before the ADC and digital cancellation after it.
  • Sub-band full duplex in 5G-Advanced, full-duplex relays and monostatic ISAC are the nearest applications.

Check your understanding

Why must analog cancellation happen before the ADC?

Without it, the self-interference is so strong that it saturates the ADC, and the weak wanted signal is lost in quantisation noise.

A radio transmits at 20 dBm with a −95 dBm noise floor. How much suppression brings self-interference down to the noise?

20 − (−95) = 115 dB.

What does sub-band full duplex change compared with TDD?

Within one TDD carrier, the base station receives uplink on a sub-band while transmitting downlink on the rest, so both directions run at the same time.
