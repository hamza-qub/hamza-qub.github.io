Visible light communication

LED lights can flicker data faster than the eye can see, turning every lamp into a wireless access point with hundreds of terahertz of free spectrum.

Light as a radio

Visible light is an electromagnetic wave like radio, just at a few hundred terahertz. That band is about a thousand times wider than all the radio spectrum below 300 GHz, and nobody needs a licence to use it.

How an LED sends data

LEDs can switch on and off millions of times a second, far faster than the eye can follow. Vary the brightness with the data, and a photodiode on the other side turns light back into current, and current back into bits.

Key idea

Light communication uses intensity modulation with direct detection: the data changes how bright the light is, not its phase. The receiver measures brightness only.

More detail: optical modulation

Because the signal is an intensity, it must be real and non-negative. Optical OFDM schemes make the time signal real with Hermitian symmetry, then make it positive by adding a DC bias (DCO-OFDM) or by clipping half of it (ACO-OFDM). White phosphor LEDs only respond up to a few megahertz unless the slow yellow phosphor is filtered out; laser diodes and micro-LEDs reach far higher bandwidths.

The optical channel

Indoors, most of the signal travels straight from the LED to the receiver. How much arrives depends on distance and on the angles at the LED and at the photodiode.

Try it: light up a room

Change the LED’s beam, its height and the receiver’s field of view, and see where the signal is strong.

Things to try:

  • Narrow the LED to 20°. The spot gets small and bright: good for one desk, bad for the room.
  • Narrow the receiver’s field of view. Light from steep angles is cut off, which also rejects interference from other lamps and sunlight.

Strengths and limits

  • strengthHuge free bandHundreds of terahertz, unlicensed, alongside lighting that is installed anyway.
  • strengthContainedLight doesn’t pass through walls: secure, and no interference between rooms.
  • strengthNo radio interferenceUsable where radio is restricted, such as in hospitals, aircraft and mines.
  • limitNeeds a clear viewBlocked light means a lost link; sunlight adds noise.
  • limitUplinkPhones can’t easily shine light back; infrared is used instead.
  • limitLED speedOrdinary white LEDs are slow; faster sources cost more.

Li-Fi is the networking version of VLC: bidirectional, multi-user and with handover between lamps. The IEEE 802.11bb standard, published in 2023, brings light communication into the Wi-Fi family.

Key takeaways
  • Visible light offers hundreds of terahertz of unlicensed spectrum.
  • LEDs send data by varying brightness; photodiodes detect it (intensity modulation, direct detection).
  • Indoors the line-of-sight path dominates, shaped by the LED’s beam and the receiver’s field of view.
  • Li-Fi and IEEE 802.11bb add networking; line of sight, sunlight and the uplink are the main limits.

Check your understanding

Why can’t VLC use ordinary complex-valued QAM directly?

The LED transmits an intensity, which must be real and non-negative, so signals are made real (Hermitian symmetry) and positive (a DC bias or clipping).

Why is Li-Fi considered secure?

Light doesn’t pass through walls, so a link can’t be overheard from the next room.

Why do many Li-Fi systems use infrared for the uplink?

A device shining visible light back towards the ceiling would dazzle users; infrared is invisible.
