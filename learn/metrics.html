The idea in 30 seconds

QualitySNR and SINR say how clean the received signal is.
QuantityThroughput, spectral efficiency and area capacity measure how much.
DependabilityOutage, latency and reliability measure how consistently.

SNR and SINR

In a network, your phone hears more than its own base station. Other cells on the same frequency add interference, which the receiver treats like extra noise.

Errors: bits, blocks and packets

Errors are counted at several levels. Bits are grouped into coded blocks, and a block either decodes correctly or doesn’t.

  • BERBit error rateShare of bits received wrongly, before or after decoding.
  • BLERBlock error rateShare of coded blocks that fail. 4G and 5G adapt the modulation and coding to aim for about 10% at the first attempt.
  • HARQRetransmissionsFailed blocks are resent (HARQ), trading latency for reliability.

Rate, efficiency and capacity

A link’s speed can be described in several ways, each normalised differently. Mixing them up is a common source of confusion.

  • bit/sThroughputbit/s actually delivered. What a speed test measures.
  • bit/s/HzSpectral efficiencybit/s per hertz. Lets you compare systems with different bandwidths.
  • bit/s/m²Area traffic capacitybit/s per square metre. Captures how densely a network can serve.
More detail: ergodic and outage capacity

In a fading channel the instantaneous capacity, , is a random variable. Two summaries are used:

  • Ergodic capacity, its average , achievable when codewords span many fading states.
  • Outage probability, , the chance that a fixed target rate R can’t be supported. It matters when delay limits how long codes can be.

Try it: outage versus average

Fading makes the capacity jump around. Pick a target rate and see how often the channel falls short, and how much worse a Rayleigh channel is than a milder one.

Things to try:

  • At 10 dB and a target of 2 bit/s/Hz, the Rayleigh channel is in outage about 26% of the time, even though its average capacity is above 2.9.
  • Switch to m = 4, a channel with a strong direct path. The curve steepens and the outage drops sharply.
  • Raise the SNR by 10 dB. In Rayleigh fading the outage falls about tenfold: the diversity order is 1.

Energy efficiency

Data rate grows only logarithmically with power, while energy use grows linearly. So there is a best transmit power, beyond which each extra watt buys very little.

Latency and reliability

For machines, when data arrives matters as much as how much. Each generation has tightened the latency target.

Glossary

Terms you will meet in papers and standards, in one place.

Key takeaways
  • SINR adds interference to the noise; with many cells, networks become interference limited.
  • Throughput, spectral efficiency and area capacity normalise speed in different ways.
  • In fading, ergodic capacity describes the average and outage probability the bad moments.
  • Energy efficiency peaks at a moderate transmit power; latency and reliability matter most for machines.

Check your understanding

A user has S = −80 dBm, I = −85 dBm and N = −95 dBm. What is the SINR?

I + N ≈ −84.6 dBm (−85 dBm plus −95 dBm in linear units), so the SINR is about 4.6 dB.

Why can’t a network raise every base station’s power to beat interference?

Interference rises in proportion, so the SINR approaches a fixed signal-to-interference ratio.

When is outage probability more meaningful than ergodic capacity?

When delay constraints prevent coding across many fading states, so each block either fits the channel or fails.
