6G technologies

The physical-layer ideas behind 6G: networks that sense, surfaces that shape the channel, radios that talk and listen at once, new spectrum, light, and new ways to share it.

  • 9 tutorials
  • Diagrams, interactive models and self-checks

The physical-layer ideas behind 6G, one per tutorial. Start with any of them; each links to the others where they meet. Below the cards: what 6G is meant to deliver and where the standard stands.

  • 1. ISACIntegrated sensing and communicationOne radio, one band and often one signal that both carries data and senses the surroundings, turning the network into a radar.10 min · model: Resolve two targets
  • 2. RISReconfigurable intelligent surfacesThin panels of many small elements, each able to switch how it reflects. Together they let the network shape the channel, which engineers could once only measure.10 min · model: Steer a reflected beam
  • 3. Full-duplexFull-duplex radiosTransmitting and receiving on the same frequency at the same time could double spectral efficiency, if a radio can stop hearing itself.8 min · model: Cancellation budget
  • 4. THzTerahertz communicationAbove 100 GHz lie tens of gigahertz of unused spectrum: enough for links of hundreds of gigabits per second, if the losses and the hardware can be tamed.8 min
  • 5. VLCVisible light communicationLED lights can flicker data faster than the eye can see, turning every lamp into a wireless access point with hundreds of terahertz of free spectrum.7 min · model: Light up a room
  • 6. RadarRadar signal processingSend a signal, listen for the echo, and turn delays, Doppler shifts and phase differences into a map of what is out there.14 min · model: Tune a CFAR detector
  • 7. NOMA & RSMANOMA and rate-splittingLet several users share the same time and frequency by giving them different power levels, then peel their signals apart at the receiver.10 min · model: Compare NOMA and TDMA rate regions
  • 8. Fluid antennasFluid and movable antennasAn antenna that changes its position within a small space. The channel changes over a fraction of a wavelength, so that freedom buys diversity without extra radio chains.9 min · model: Pick the best port
  • 9. VehicularVehicular communicationsVehicles talking to each other, to the roadside, to pedestrians and to the network. Speed is what makes it hard: the channel can change in a fraction of a millisecond.9 min · model: See Doppler fading

What the ITU asks of 6G

The ITU defines each generation through an IMT framework: IMT-2020 for 5G, IMT-2030 for 6G (Recommendation ITU-R M.2160, November 2023).
