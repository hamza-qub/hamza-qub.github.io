One radio, two jobs

Radar and communication grew up as separate systems. Today both use wide bandwidths, big antenna arrays and digital processing, so they can share spectrum, hardware and even the same signal.

ISAC is one of the six usage scenarios of IMT-2030, the ITU framework for 6G. 6G overview

Levels of integration

Integration ranges from simply sharing the band to a single waveform that does both jobs.

What a radio can measure

A target reflects part of the signal. From the echo, the receiver estimates three things.

Key idea

The signal’s bandwidth sets the range resolution: the closest two targets can be and still appear as two.

A 100 MHz 5G carrier resolves about 1.5 m; a 4 GHz automotive radar sweep about 3.7 cm.

Things to try:

  • Set the separation to 1 m and raise the bandwidth from 50 to 400 MHz. The single blurred peak splits in two.
  • At 10 MHz, even 10 m apart is not enough. Narrowband systems can’t tell nearby objects apart, however strong the echo.

The trade-off

Sensing wants known waveforms and beams that sweep the scene. Communication wants random data symbols and beams aimed at users. In one design they compete for the same power, time, bandwidth and beams.

Key idea

A network that senses with its own data signals faces a subtle cost: the data are random. Constellations whose points all have the same amplitude, such as QPSK, give clean range profiles; higher-order QAM carries more bits but its varying amplitudes raise random sidelobes. Liu, Masouros, Eldar and co-authors call this the deterministic–random trade-off.

Sensing for security

A broadcast also reaches eavesdroppers. Jamming them works only if you know where they are, and usually you don’t. Sensing can fill that gap.

Open problems

  • Self-interferenceA monostatic receiver must hear weak echoes over its own transmission.
  • WaveformsOFDM suits data and works for sensing, but its peaks and sidelobes cost performance.
  • SynchronisationBistatic and multistatic sensing need tightly aligned clocks.
  • PrivacyA network that can sense people raises questions of consent.
Key takeaways
  • ISAC shares spectrum, hardware and sometimes the waveform between sensing and communication.
  • Range resolution is c/2B, so wide bandwidths make good sensors.
  • Sensing and communication compete for resources; joint designs reach a Pareto boundary that time sharing cannot.
  • Sensing can support security: locate the threat, then jam it.

Check your understanding

What bandwidth gives a range resolution of 30 cm?

B = c/(2Δr) = 3×10⁸/0.6 = 500 MHz.

Why is monostatic ISAC harder to build than bistatic?

The transmitter and receiver sit together, so the strong transmitted signal leaks into the receiver while it listens for weak echoes.

Why must a sensing-assisted jammer be robust?

The eavesdropper’s estimated position has errors, so jamming aimed at one point can miss; it must cover the region where the eavesdropper could be.
